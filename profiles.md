**Yunzhi Zhang** | PhD student | yzzhang@cs.stanford.edu

Prior to Stanford she did research at BAIR under Abbeel and Pinto. Her work is a near-exact match to your representation learning research.
- Papers: [The Scene Language (CVPR 2025)](https://ai.stanford.edu/~yzzhang/projects/scene-language/) | [3D Congealing (ECCV 2025)](https://cs.stanford.edu/~yzzhang/)

**Chen Geng** | PhD student | gengchen@cs.stanford.edu

Geng's CVPR 2025 paper is "Birth and Death of a Rose" — modeling the full 4D lifecycle of physical phenomena — and he has a related CVPR 2025 work on 4D vision and computer graphics. His focus on learning structured physical dynamics from video maps to your JEPA / world model interest.

**Joy Hsu** | PhD student | joycj@stanford.edu
Theme: Neuro-symbolic reasoning, concept grounding, compositional representation learning.
Hsu is part of Wu's group, working on visual reasoning via physical concept grounding in the neuro-symbolic tradition (LEFT project — grounding language to structured perception). Her work sits at the junction of learning structured world representations and compositional reasoning — relevant to the "what JEPA should be learning" question in your research.
- Papers: [LEFT (NeurIPS 2023)](https://web.stanford.edu/~joycj/projects/left_neurips_2023.html) | [BEHAVIOR (CoRL 2025)](https://behavior.stanford.edu)

## 2. Sergey Levine (Berkeley) — RAIL Lab
**Lab:** https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu

---

**Homer Walke** | PhD student | hwalke@berkeley.edu
Theme: Large-scale robot learning, VLAs, dataset collection.
Walke led BridgeData V2 (CoRL 2023), a large-scale robot learning dataset, and contributed to the π0.5 Vision-Language-Action model (CoRL 2025). He also works on autonomous improvement of instruction-following via foundation models. Direct connection to data-driven generalist robotics — relevant to your interests in SSL for real-world robot generalization.
- Papers: [BridgeData V2 (CoRL 2023)](https://homerwalke.com/) | [π0.5 (CoRL 2025)](https://homerwalke.com/)

**Seohong Park** | PhD student | seohong@berkeley.edu
Theme: Scalable offline RL, goal-conditioned RL, offline-to-online learning.
Park's driving research question is whether RL is truly scalable. He introduced OGBench, a new benchmark for offline goal-conditioned RL, and Flow Q-Learning (FQL), which uses flow matching for offline RL policies. His work on offline RL scalability with expressive objectives is a strong parallel to your contrastive vs. SIGReg loss experiments.
- Papers: [OGBench](https://seohong.me/projects/ogbench) | [FQL (offline RL with flow matching)](https://seohong.me/projects/fql/)

**Kevin Frans** | PhD student (with Abbeel & Levine) | hi@kvfrans.com
Theme: Deep learning theory, self-improving systems, scalable RL, generative modeling.
Frans's interests are in understanding deep learning and scalable self-improving systems, with prior work in RL and generative modeling. He previously spent time at OpenAI on the RL team. Recent work includes one-step diffusion via shortcut models and an analysis of matrix-whitening optimizers (SPlus) for training stability.
- Papers: [Diffusion Guidance as Policy Improvement (arXiv)](https://kvfrans.com/) | [What Really Matters in Matrix-Whitening Optimizers (2025)](https://kvfrans.com/)

**Qiyang (Colin) Li** | PhD student | colinqiyangli@berkeley.edu
Theme: Generative models for RL, offline + online exploration.
Li's research focuses on leveraging expressive generative models to improve RL algorithms for both offline learning and online exploration. His framing — generative models as a structural tool for RL, not just data augmentation — overlaps with the representational questions in your contrastive SSL + RL work.
- Papers: [ICML 2025 work with Frans & Levine](https://colinqiyangli.github.io/) | [Generative model-based RL exploration](https://colinqiyangli.github.io/)

**Vivek Myers** | PhD student (with Dragan & Levine) | vmyers@berkeley.edu
Theme: RL, human-AI interaction, robot goal specification.
Myers works on reinforcement learning, human-AI interaction, and robotics with NDSEG fellowship support. His work on semi-supervised language interfaces for robot control (CoRL 2023) is directly relevant if you're thinking about how to ground SSL representations in language-conditioned tasks.
- Papers: [Goal Representations for Instruction Following (CoRL 2023)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~vmyers/) | [Stabilizing Contrastive RL (ICLR 2024 Spotlight)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~vmyers/)

**Jonathan Yang** | PhD student

**Moo Jin Kim** | PhD student | mj-kim@stanford.edu

## 4. Angjoo Kanazawa (Berkeley) — KAIR Lab
**Lab:** https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~kanazawa/

---

**Hongsuk Choi** | PhD student | hongsukchoi@berkeley.edu
Theme: Humanoid control from video, 4D human-scene reconstruction.
Choi is a co-first author on VideoMimic (CoRL 2025, Best Student Paper Award) — a system that reconstructs 3D environments and human motion from single-camera videos for humanoid imitation learning. He also co-authored a CVPR 2025 Highlight on reconstructing people, places, and cameras. This is a direct intersection of visual perception and humanoid robot control, which maps well onto your depth + SSL + RL stack.
- Papers: [VideoMimic (CoRL 2025 Best Student Paper)](https://hongsukchoi.github.io/) | [Reconstructing People, Places, Cameras (CVPR 2025)](https://hongsukchoi.github.io/)

**Chung Min Kim** | PhD student
Theme: Robot kinematics, kinematic optimization tools, contact-aware manipulation.
Kim co-led PyRoki (IROS 2025), a modular, cross-platform toolkit for robot kinematic optimization, supporting inverse kinematics, trajectory optimization, and motion retargeting across CPU/GPU/TPU. Also contributed to VideoMimic and Eye, Robot.
- Papers: [PyRoki (IROS 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03728) | [Eye, Robot (CoRL 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10968)

**Brent Yi** | PhD student
Theme: Egocentric body estimation, camera-relative pose, 3D world reconstruction.
Yi led EgoAllo (CVPR 2025), which estimates body and hand motion in an ego-sensed world using egocentric SLAM poses and a conditional diffusion model, with a novel parameterization for spatial and temporal invariance. His work on learning geometry-aware representations from egocentric video is directly relevant to your depth + SSL interest.
- Papers: [EgoAllo (CVPR 2025)](https://egoallo.github.io/) | [Cameras as Relative Positional Encoding (arXiv 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10496)

**David McAllister** | PhD student
Theme: Flow-based RL, generative policies, score distillation.
McAllister introduced Flow Policy Optimization (FPO, arXiv 2025), an on-policy RL algorithm that integrates flow matching into the policy gradient framework, enabling multimodal action distributions without recursive backpropagation. Also contributed to VideoMimic. The combination of generative models and RL connects directly to your contrastive + generative model ablation work.
- Papers: [Flow Policy Optimization (arXiv 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21053) | [VideoMimic (CoRL 2025)](https://hongsukchoi.github.io/VideoMimic)

**Justin Kerr** | PhD student
Theme: Perception-action loops, active vision for robot manipulation.
Kerr co-led "Eye, Robot" (CoRL 2025) — studying how to jointly learn to look and act using a BC-RL perception-action loop — and earlier "Robot See Robot Do" (CoRL 2024), which reconstructs articulated object manipulation from monocular 4D video for robot imitation.
- Papers: [Eye, Robot (CoRL 2025)](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10968) | [Robot See Robot Do (CoRL 2024)](https://robot-see-robot-do.github.io/)
